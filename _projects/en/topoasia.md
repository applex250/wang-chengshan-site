**TopoAsia**, initiated by Academician Wang Chengshan together with scientists from India, Russia and other countries and jointly launched by the Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences, China University of Geosciences (Beijing) and Chengdu University of Technology, aims to build an open, shared and collaborative international research platform for the systematic study of the **four-dimensional topography** (three-dimensional space plus time) of the Asian continent, from the deep mantle to surface processes. Asia, with the most complex tectonic framework and the most active surface processes on Earth, is a natural laboratory for studying the evolution of the Earth system.

The programme was officially launched in **Shanghai in October 2025** and runs from **2025 to 2031**. Its first international symposium was held on 21–23 October 2025, where more than 250 experts and scholars from nearly 30 countries witnessed the launch and signed the milestone **TopoAsia Shanghai Declaration**. The symposium distilled six priority scientific questions and identified four core "natural laboratories": the Pacific–Indonesian margin system, the Himalaya–Tibet–Indochina system, the Asian Craton system and the Central Asian Orogenic Belt.

TopoAsia is formally supported by the International Lithosphere Programme (ILP), the International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS), the International Union of Geodesy and Geophysics (IUGG), the Asia Oceania Geosciences Society (AOGS) and the International Association of Palaeogeography (IAP), and has been incorporated into the ILP Coordinating Committee's projects. On **9 July 2026**, the programme was formally endorsed under the third batch of the UNESCO **International Decade of Sciences for Sustainable Development (IDSSD, 2024–2033)** — the only international cooperation programme in that list focused on deep-time geography and the four-dimensional evolution of the Earth system.

Guided by the principles of "joint consultation, joint construction, shared benefits and win-win outcomes", the programme focuses on deep–shallow coupling mechanisms and, through a unified open data platform and international cooperation mechanisms, reaches out to Belt and Road partner countries and the Global South, attracting global contributors via open data and a three-tier authorship mechanism.
